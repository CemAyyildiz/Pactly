## Findings

Each finding carries its source and two dispositions: what to do about this instance, and what would prevent the next one.

### F-1 — The epic's own change record is 89% noise it cannot use

**Source:** table above; `contracts/escrow/test_snapshots/` (79 files, 42,652 lines); `spec-1-5-attempted-implementation.patch` (8,056 lines); lockfiles (4,404).

Generated snapshots, a stored patch and lockfiles are 89.4% of the epic's added lines. Contract source — the thing the epic exists to produce — is 4.5%. The snapshots are not verification: soroban-sdk's `Env` writes them on drop and nothing ever reads one back, established by Story 1.4's verification-gap layer and never contested since.

This is not cosmetic. Every review layer of Stories 1.4, 1.5 and 1.7 read those files as part of the diff under review; Story 1.5's review diff was 667 KB of which 595 KB was snapshots. The cost was paid four times per story until it was measured and excluded during Story 1.7.

**This instance:** accept — the files are committed and harmless where they sit.
**Prevent the next:** the review diff must exclude generated paths by default, not by remembering. Recorded as an action item.

### F-2 — Deferred work has two homes and neither is authoritative

**Source:** 17 items across story-spec `deferred:` frontmatter (1.4 ×6, 1.5 ×6, 1.6 ×1, 1.7 ×4); `deferred-work.md` carries 5.

Thirteen of the seventeen per-story deferred items never reach `deferred-work.md`, the file a human would open to find outstanding work. Two of `deferred-work.md`'s five entries describe work that Story 1.3 completed and were never closed. The snapshot-churn item appears three times (1.4, 1.5, 1.7) because nothing dedupes across stories, and Story 1.4's "non-conforming token contract" item appears nowhere afterwards — it simply dropped.

**This instance:** fix now — reconcile the two lists, close what is done, carry forward what is not.
**Prevent the next:** one list, with the story specs pointing at it rather than duplicating it.

### F-3 — The cancellation model was wrong for three stories before anything caught it

**Source:** `spec-1-5-resolving-a-cancellation.md` (status `blocked`, intent gap); `sprint-change-proposal-2026-09-18.md`; PRD FR7 and AD-2 as amended in `3ab7000`.

Stories 1.2, 1.3 and 1.4 were written, implemented and reviewed against a model where a permissionless `resolve_cancel` decided the outcome by the ledger clock alone. That model paid the professional for a session the professional had themselves cancelled, because FR7 treated every session that did not happen as the client's doing. No professional-cancels path existed anywhere in the PRD, the functional requirements or the architecture.

It surfaced only at Story 1.5's review, and only because the intent-alignment layer cross-read the UX, the PRD and `types.rs` and noticed `cancel_deadline` is the end of the free-cancellation window, not the session time. The story was halted, its implementation reverted and preserved as a patch, and the model replaced across the PRD, the architecture and the UX.

**What this cost:** one story's full implementation discarded, and the amendment of two planning documents mid-epic.
**What it bought:** the escrow does not pay a provider for cancelling. Catching it at 1.5 rather than in Epic 3 is the cheapest place it could have been caught, short of planning.

**This instance:** accept — resolved, and the resolution is recorded in the proposal and the amended documents.
**Prevent the next:** the gap was in the captured intent, not the implementation. A money-path story's intent deserves an explicit "who can be harmed by this, and does the model let them be" pass at planning, not at review.

### F-4 — The story specs' testing constraint did not name its level, and three mutations survived because of it

**Source:** `spec-1-7-testnet-setup-scripts.md` Always list ("The logic that does not touch the network … is pure and unit-tested. Network calls stay thin wrappers around it"); Story 1.7 triage log, the `main()` entry.

Seven of Story 1.7's nine I/O-matrix rows are stated about the *command*; the constraint was read — defensibly — as "test the leaf functions". The result was that `setup-testnet.ts` exported nothing and none of its behaviour was reachable from a test. Three mutations survived the whole suite: deleting the admin-wallet assignment, neutering the testnet guard, and changing the asset code the anchor resolution matches on.

The same shape appears in Story 1.4, where nothing pinned that `release` ignores the deadline, and in Story 1.5, where nothing pinned the auth-before-state ordering on the three new paths. In all three cases the verification-gap layer found it by mutation, not by reading.

**This instance:** fixed in each story.
**Prevent the next:** when a spec's matrix states rows about a command or an entry point, the testing constraint must say tests reach that surface. Recorded as an action item.

### F-5 — Story 1.6 produced no code, and that was predictable at planning time

**Source:** `spec-1-6-contract-unit-tests.md`; commit `3e6911c`.

Story 1.6's eight acceptance criteria were all already satisfied by the 62 tests Stories 1.2–1.5 had written, because each of those stories had to test its own I/O matrix. The story was closed with an audit mapping each criterion to the test that proves it, and no code.

This is a good outcome — the alternative was writing duplicate tests to justify the story — but it means the epic carried a story whose work had been absorbed elsewhere, and nobody noticed until it was reached.

**This instance:** accept — closed honestly with an audit trail.
**Prevent the next:** a "write the tests" story placed after the stories that must test themselves is usually already spent. Either place it first as a testing-strategy story, or plan it as the gap-closing story it actually becomes.

### F-6 — Snapshot churn was deferred four times and is still open

**Source:** `deferred-work.md` (logged from Story 1.3); deferred entries in 1.4, 1.5, 1.7; three manual `git checkout` cleanups during Story 1.7 alone.

Two Story 1.2 tests use `BytesN::<16>::random`, so `cargo test` rewrites their committed snapshots on every run. It is one line per test. It has been deferred in four consecutive stories, and by Story 1.7 it was dirtying the working tree of a story that does not touch `contracts/` at all.

**This instance:** fix now — it is two lines and it has cost more in repeated cleanup than the fix.
**Prevent the next:** a deferred item that recurs in three stories is not a deferral, it is an unowned bug. Worth a rule.

### F-7 — Story status in sprint tracking was never advanced past `review`

**Source:** `sprint-status.yaml` before this retrospective; its own header comment ("Dev moves story to 'review', then runs code-review").

All seven stories sat at `review` with their specs `done`. The file documents the `review` step but never says who moves a story to `done`, so nobody did — and the retrospective's completeness gate would have forced a **rejected** verdict on seven stories that were finished.

**This instance:** fixed before the analysis, with the user's agreement.
**Prevent the next:** whichever step closes review should also advance the story key, or the status vocabulary should drop the state nobody writes.

### Cross-story boundary findings

A review pass scoped to the seams between stories — the ground no single story's review could see. Every claim below was re-checked against the primary source before being routed; the ones that did not hold up are not listed.

### F-8 — `types.rs` still describes the two-outcome model, and Epic 2 reads it

**Source:** `contracts/escrow/src/types.rs:38` ("receives it back on an on-time cancellation") and `:44` ("before it the client may cancel for a refund"); `git diff 1f81e29 HEAD -- types.rs` is empty.

Story 1.5 replaced the settlement model and never touched `types.rs`, so the file the epic context names as the shared data-model authority still describes the model it replaced. The client is now also made whole by `cancel_by_professional`, at any time — a second, unrelated way that field gets paid, which the doc does not mention. Line 44 is also off by one second against `lib.rs`'s deliberate `<=`.

**This instance:** fix now. **Prevent the next:** a model change should sweep the data-model docs, not only the call sites.

### F-9 — The deadline bound was narrowed in 1.3 and two files still state the old rule

**Source:** `contracts/escrow/src/storage.rs:9-11` ("further out than `BUMP_LEDGERS` worth of time from now"); `contracts/escrow/src/error.rs:25-27` ("further out than a stored booking's TTL"); as built, `lib.rs:40-41` subtracts a full `SETTLEMENT_MARGIN_SECONDS`.

The real bound is 90 days, not 120. This rule spans three stories — 1.2 owns the TTL, 1.3 owns the bound, 1.5's `claim_no_show` is why the margin exists — and the two files a new contributor opens first still describe the version that would strand a deposit.

**This instance:** fix now. **Prevent the next:** same as F-8.

### F-10 — The error catalogue promises a check the contract does not make

**Source:** `contracts/escrow/src/error.rs:28-29` — `InvalidParties` is "The professional and the client are the same address, **or a party is the escrow contract itself**"; `lib.rs:132` tests only `professional == client || professional == escrow`. `client == escrow` is never checked.

Not exploitable — an external caller cannot satisfy `require_auth` for the contract's own address — but the error catalogue is the off-chain contract Epic 2 maps to user-facing messages, so an over-broad description is a liability rather than a nit.

**This instance:** fix now — narrow the doc to what is enforced, or enforce what it says. **Prevent the next:** error docs are API surface; review them as such.

### F-11 — Two helpers answer the same question, one per story

**Source:** `assert_no_effect(f, funding)` at `contracts/escrow/src/test.rs:836` (Story 1.3) and `assert_nothing_changed(f, before)` at `:881` (Story 1.4), carrying near-identical doc paragraphs. The `Snapshot` struct already covers everything the older helper asserts, plus the professional's balance, which it omits. Relatedly, `const FUNDING` is declared under the Story 1.4 banner while the 1.3 section above repeats the bare literal twenty times.

Story 1.6 was the natural consolidating pass and closed as already satisfied, so nothing reconciled them.

**This instance:** defer — cosmetic, and the weaker helper is not wrong, only narrower. **Prevent the next:** see F-5.

### F-12 — The scripts workspace sits outside the epic's own gates

**Source:** `scripts/package.json` defines `setup:testnet`, `typecheck`, `test` — no `build`; root `package.json:16` runs `npm run build --workspaces --if-present`, so `scripts` is skipped. Its tests run through `tsx`, which strips types without checking them. The mirror image exists in the backend: `backend/tsconfig.json` has `"include": ["src"]`, so `backend/test/` is never type-checked, while `scripts/tsconfig.json` includes `test`.

Net effect: Story 1.7's TypeScript is type-checked by no command the README documents, and the backend's tests by none at all. Two workspaces, opposite halves of the same convention, neither complete.

**This instance:** fix now — one command that type-checks everything. **Prevent the next:** a new workspace should inherit the gates, not re-declare a subset.

### F-13 — Nothing enforces formatting or lint anywhere in the repo

**Source:** `rust-toolchain.toml` pins `profile = "minimal"`, so `cargo fmt` is not installed; no eslint, prettier, editorconfig or CI configuration exists. Visible symptom: `contracts/escrow/src/test.rs:13-15`, where a second `use crate::storage::…` was appended below `use crate::types::…`, out of the order rustfmt would impose.

Style consistency across seven sessions rests entirely on review.

**This instance:** defer — a hackathon repo can live without it. **Prevent the next:** if Epic 2 adds three more workspaces, this stops being free.

### F-14 — The "one command" setup leaves the contract unusable, and no document gives the missing command

**Source:** `create_booking` returns `NotInitialized` until `initialize` runs (`lib.rs:104-106`); `scripts/src/setup-testnet.ts` deploys and stops. The only acknowledgement is prose in `scripts/README.md` calling it "a separate, deliberate step" — with no command and no guidance on which address to pass. The root `README.md` does not mention it at all. The epic's own constraint reads *"Setup must be reproducible with one command so demo day loses no time to environment work."*

Compounding it, `demo-accounts.ts` correctly notes that the contract's `admin` is a different concept from `PACTLY_ADMIN_WALLETS`, so an operator cannot even infer which address to use.

**This instance:** fix now — this is the epic's headline constraint, missed at the last step.
**Prevent the next:** the acceptance criterion said "funds, trustlines, deploys"; nobody asked what the contract needs before it will accept a call.

### F-15 — `claim_no_show` has no consumer story anywhere

**Source:** `claim_no_show` appears in `prd.md:222` (Story 1.5's own AC) and nowhere else — not in Story 3.6's amended criteria, not in `EXPERIENCE.md`. Yet AD-2 names "the provider being stuck when a client no-shows" as a prevented failure, and that is the PRD's headline problem statement.

The mid-epic amendment replaced one function with three and gave UX coverage to two of them.

**This instance:** fix now — propose the Story 3.6 criterion. **Prevent the next:** when a course correction changes a contract's surface, walk every downstream story, not only the ones the diff touched.

## Behavior verification

**The contract:** exercised only through its unit tests — 62 tests, all passing, against a natively registered contract. The `wasm32v1-none` artifact builds cleanly but **no test exercises it**, so the event XDR shape, the error discriminants on the wire and the host's auth enforcement are verified by reading and by the native harness, never against the artifact that would be deployed. Carried as a deferred item in `spec-1-6`.

**The testnet scripts:** **not exercised at all.** Funding, trustline and deploy are irreversible outward actions; the spec forbade running them and the Stellar CLI is deliberately not installed on this machine. 69 unit tests cover the decision logic and the failure paths through injected seams. Three of Story 1.7's four high-severity fixes — the secret moved out of argv, the contract-id validation, the CLI-check ordering — live on that unexecuted path.

**Nothing in this epic has run against Stellar testnet.** That is the single largest gap between what is verified and what will be demonstrated.

## Acceptance verdict

**accepted-with-open-items.** Criteria were **declared**, in `epic-1-context.md`'s Goal and Requirements & Constraints.

**What the criteria asked, and what the evidence shows:**

| Declared criterion | Evidence | Met |
|---|---|---|
| A deposit can be locked | `create_booking`, `deposit_is_released_and_the_professional_is_paid` and its locking siblings | Yes |
| …released | `release`, tested | Yes |
| …refunded | `cancel_by_professional`, `cancel_by_client` in-window, tested | Yes |
| …transferred (no-show) | `claim_no_show`, late `cancel_by_client`, tested | Yes |
| Every path proven by unit tests | 62 contract tests, all passing | Yes |
| `cargo test` must pass | 62 passed, 0 failed | Yes |
| Setup reproducible with one command | Command exists, 69 unit tests, **never executed**; and it stops short of `initialize` (F-14) | **Partly** |

Six of the seven declared criteria are demonstrably met. The seventh — the one-command setup — is written and unit-tested but has never run, and as built it leaves the contract uninitialised, so "reproducible" is unproven in both senses.

No blocking finding stands unresolved: the one defect that would have been blocking, the cancellation model paying a provider for cancelling (F-3), was caught during the epic and fixed across the code and the planning documents. All seven stories are `done`; `pending_stories` is empty.

This is **accepted-with-open-items** rather than accepted because action items 1 and 2 are named, tracked, and must close before the epic's own stated purpose — losing no demo time to environment work — can be claimed.

A human decision overrides this verdict. None was recorded; the call above is the machine's, on the evidence.

## Open questions

- **Does the setup command actually work?** Nothing in this epic has run against Stellar testnet. The first real run is the only thing that can answer it, and it will test three high-severity fixes at once. Requires the Stellar CLI, which is deliberately not installed here.
- **Should the professional's no-show claim reach the UI in Epic 3, or is it a backend/cron concern?** AD-2 justifies the path by the provider not being stranded, but nothing says who triggers it in practice. The answer changes whether item 7 is a UI story or an operations one.
- **Is a professional who simply does not appear a problem this product will solve?** Recorded as a `medium` deferral on Story 1.5: after the free-cancellation window closes, every path the client can sign pays the professional, and the contract cannot tell who was absent. Closing it needs mutual confirmation or a dispute mechanism — both explicitly out of Epic 1's scope, and neither is on any epic's plan.
- **Were there process lessons the artifacts cannot show?** No session logs exist on disk for this epic's stories, so every process finding here is inferred from triage logs, spec change history and commits. A lesson that left no artifact is invisible to this retrospective.
